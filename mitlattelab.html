Making Financial Literacy Feel Like an Invitation, Not a Lecture

Type: Brand Identity, Event Design, Campaign Design

House Fund

HouseFund is a financial literacy event designed for college students. Inspired by the familiarity and social energy of a house party, the brand reframes personal finance as something approachable, communal, and empowering. The identity bridges the gap between financial education and Gen Z culture, creating an experience that feels welcoming rather than intimidating.

Goal

Many students:

  • Feel overwhelmed by financial topics
  • Associate finance with banks, debt, or complicated terminology
  • Lack engagement with traditional financial literacy programs

How might we make financial literacy feel as accessible and appealing as a social event while maintaining trust and educational value?

Key Insight

College students willingly attend social events, networking events, and house parties, but rarely seek out financial education events on their own.

By borrowing visual cues and language from social culture, financial literacy can become more inviting and relevant to Gen Z audiences.

Takeaways

The most important design challenge was balancing credibility with approachability. The identity needed enough energy to attract students while still establishing trust around financial education. Through the HouseFund concept, I explored how branding can reshape perceptions of traditionally intimidating subjects and create stronger audience engagement.
